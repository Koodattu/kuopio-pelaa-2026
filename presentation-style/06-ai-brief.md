# 06 · Briefs for a designer or an AI tool

## Short style brief

> A Finnish-language talk for curious non-experts about `<topic>`. Frame
> slides (opening, agenda, summary, closing) are scrapbook collage:
> corkboard or cardboard background, ransom-note cut-out title letters,
> pastel sticky notes held by push pins, a cut-out photo of the speaker and a
> cardboard robot mascot holding `<topic props>`. Content slides are clean
> warm off-white paper (`#F1ECE1`) with a heavy black Arial Black title
> top-left, a grey one-line subtitle, the evidence in the middle and a small
> "Lähde:" source line bottom-left. Each slide makes one claim or asks one
> question, backs it with a chart, quote card or screenshot, and ends with a
> bold one-line punchline or a question to the room. Use progressive chart
> reveals, real news cases told in 3–4 beats, "wait, didn't they just
> say…?" contradictions, historical reframes and occasional full-slide memes.
> Tone: curious and sceptical, self-ironic, never preachy — give criteria, not
> verdicts. End with a hands-on workshop.

## Outline a new talk

> Using the style brief above and the arc below, draft an outline for a
> `<length>`-minute talk on `<topic>` for `<audience>`. The title should follow
> the pattern "`<dramatic claim>`… vai onko?". For each beat give: slide title,
> subtitle, the evidence I need to find (be specific about what kind of
> source), and the bottom line. Mark where the audience is asked something.
> Keep to ~1 point per 45 seconds. Do not invent statistics; write
> `[FIND: …]` where data is needed.
>
> Arc: hook → who I am → word-association warm-up → basics and why now →
> what professionals think → what users think → why opinions differ → two
> controversy cases + framework → domain by domain → what's possible now →
> "been here before" → opportunity → answer the title → my own project →
> three takeaways → workshop.

## Generate collage assets

Title word (one image per word, transparent background):

> A single word "`<WORD>`" made of mismatched ransom-note letters cut from
> magazines and newspapers: each letter a different font, colour and paper
> scrap, slightly rotated, with paper edges and a soft drop shadow.
> Transparent background, no other objects.

Mascot:

> A cut-out collage character: a robot built from brown cardboard boxes with
> an old beige CRT monitor as a head showing an angry red pixel face, several
> flexible duct-hose arms holding `<prop 1>`, `<prop 2>`, `<prop 3>`,
> `<prop 4>`. White sticker outline, transparent background, photographic
> paper-craft style.

Speaker cut-out:

> Remove the background from this photo and add a thick white sticker-style
> outline and a soft drop shadow, as if cut out with scissors and taped onto a
> corkboard.

Remember to disclose generated illustrations in the deck.

## Review an existing deck

> Review this deck against the style guide in `presentation-style/`. For each
> slide, say whether it should be kept, merged, moved, reworked or dropped,
> and why. Check specifically: slide budget for `<length>` minutes, repeated
> points or statistics, sources that don't support their slide, charts whose
> headline overstates the data, tangents, foundation material placed after
> the demos it explains, more than one summary, unreadable screenshots,
> undisclosed AI illustrations, and Finnish spelling. Do not edit the file.
