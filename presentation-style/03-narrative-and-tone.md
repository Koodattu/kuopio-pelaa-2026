# 03 · Narrative and tone

## The stance

- **Curious sceptic, not a preacher.** Enthusiasm and criticism are both
  visible. The talk never tells the audience what to think about the topic;
  it gives them criteria to think with.
- **Insider credibility.** The speaker has built something real in the area
  and shows it. Commentary is backed by practice.
- **The audience is smart but not specialist.** No prior expertise needed;
  jargon is introduced once with a concrete example.

## The title

A provocative claim with built-in doubt:

> Tekoäly tuli – nyt kaikki on ohi… **vai onko?**

Pattern: `<dramatic claim the audience half-believes> … vai onko?` /
`… vai?` / `… oikeasti?`. The second half promises the talk will complicate
the claim, not confirm it. **The talk must answer the title explicitly** near
the end.

## Recurring narrative moves

| Move | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Guess first** | Show an empty chart, let the room guess, then reveal. People remember data they predicted. | GDC sentiment 2024 → 2025 → 2026 |
| **News story in 3–4 beats** | Hype → discovery → response → "did it matter?" Makes an abstract issue concrete. | 1666: Amsterdam, Clair Obscur |
| **"Hetkinen…"** | Set a statement next to an action that contradicts it. Deadpan, not gloating. | "Ei generatiivista AI:ta" vs. a trailer made with it |
| **Then a framework** | After two or three cases, give the tool for judging them. | "Mitä 'ei tekoälyä' oikeastaan lupaa?" (three levels) |
| **Domain by domain** | Walk through the sub-areas; for each: what it can already do, where it falls short. | Ohjelmointi, visuaalinen taide, ääni, käsikirjoitus, pelisuunnittelu |
| **Show, then ask** | Demos first, opinion question after. | "Mitä näistä pelaisit? Mihin näistä palaisit?" |
| **"Ollaanko tässä oltu ennenkin?"** | Reframe the new thing as an old pattern with a new tool. Lowers panic, sharpens the real question. | Asset flips, Fall Guys → Stumble Guys, hand-weavers |
| **Global/cultural contrast** | Same technology, different expectations depending on who benefits. | Ipsos country chart, China vs. USA |
| **Opportunity turn** | After the critique, the invitation. | "Nyt on paras aika rakentaa peli" |
| **Personal proof** | The speaker's own project as the worked example. | SuomiWoW |
| **Talk → do** | End with a hands-on part so the audience tests the claims themselves. | Workshop playground |

## Pacing rhythm

A section usually runs:

```
question title → evidence → evidence/twist → framework or punchline → meme
```

Memes are commas between sections, not inside an argument. One meme per joke.

## Voice

- **Language:** Finnish, spoken register in titles and punchlines, standard
  Finnish in explanations. English quotes are kept in English when the
  wording matters; otherwise translated.
- **Self-irony:** the bio ends with a joke; the speaker admits their own use
  ("Minä, jolla agentti pyörii taustalla").
- **Wordplay:** small twists on familiar phrases ("pyhä kolminaisuus" →
  "pyhä nelinäisyys"; "Sama teknologia. Hieman eri myyntipuhe.").
- **Questions to "sinä":** bottom lines often address the listener directly
  ("Minkä näistä sinä kuulit…?").
- **Short sentences.** One idea per line. If a sentence needs a comma and an
  "and", split it.

## Audience interaction

Plan these into the deck, not just the speaker notes:

1. Permission to interrupt at the start.
2. A word-association warm-up.
3. A poll or show of hands early (and optionally the same questions again at
   the end to show change).
4. "Guess first" on at least one chart.
5. Direct questions at the bottom of story slides.
6. A "would you use / play / buy this?" moment after demos.
7. The hands-on part.

## Honesty conventions

- Every claim slide has a `Lähde:` line with linked sources.
- Caveats live in the source line: sample size, self-selected survey,
  regions not representative ("Kiina ja USA eivät edusta koko Aasiaa ja
  länttä").
- Don't combine different studies into one trend line unless labelled.
- Illustrative (made-up) charts are labelled as such.
- Disclose your own use of the technology you are critiquing.
